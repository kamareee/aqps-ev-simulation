## 1. Blockers

### B1. Synthetic PV data presented as real AEMO data
**Evidence (code)**
- `scripts/prepare_aemo_pv.py` contains no network access at all: no `urllib`, no `requests`, no NEMWeb URL.
- **L143–144:** forecast and actual curves are `solar_envelope(peak, sunrise, sunset)`, a sin^1.35 curve with hand-picked peak values (for example `fcst_peak: 18.8`, `act_peak: 18.4`).
- **L146–151:** the "cloud" is a hand-placed Gaussian dip (`cloud_dip_center`, `cloud_dip_depth`) plus seeded noise, `rng.normal(1.0, hf_fluct)` with `seed: 101…106`.
- **L131:** the output is labelled `"source": "AEMO NEMWeb MMS DEMAND_FORECASTS (ROOFTOP_PV_FORECAST, ROOFTOP_PV_ACTUAL), Region VIC1"`, with an APVI cross-check named in its metadata. No APVI cross-check exists anywhere in the code.
- The docstring says "embedded verified AEMO VIC1 historical 30-min profiles". No such embedded data exists.

**Evidence (manuscript)**
- `main.tex` L347: "Rather than using synthetic Gaussian error models, solar generation is driven by historical AEMO NEMWeb MMS Data Model profiles … cross-checked against the APVI Solar Map".
- Also in L80, L554–566 (§4.6 title "Real Australian AEMO Data"), L667 (limitations), the abstract, the highlights ("AEMO solar data") and the cover letter ("historical AEMO rooftop PV forecast and actual data").
- The labels "Overcast Front" and "Afternoon Stormfront" for 2025-06-18 and 2025-08-20 are invented by the script, not taken from any weather record.

**Why it matters:** this is a false statement about where the data came from. It is a research-integrity risk regardless of how it got there. The scripts look machine-generated (each starts with "Save as: …").

**Fix (choose one)**
- (a) **Recommended.** Write a real NEMWeb download script for the chosen dates (VIC1 `ROOFTOP_PV_FORECAST` day-ahead vintage, plus `ROOFTOP_PV_ACTUAL`). Normalise to 20 kW, commit the raw files and the script, and rerun E1 and E4. You run it locally, because the Claude workspace can't reach NEMWeb.
- (b) Relabel everything as "synthetic AEMO-shaped profiles". Remove the AEMO and APVI claims, and drop "real data" from the title of §4.6, the abstract, the highlights and the cover letter.

### B3. Selective baseline ranges
**Evidence**
- Abstract and conclusion: "outperforming LLF (74.7–89.2%), EDF (69.3–89.2%) … across all contention and peak-stress scenarios".
- In 45-EV S2 (a contention scenario), LLF has 99.96% priority, 100% mission sufficiency and 100% non-priority fulfilment. AQPS-RT-ET has 95.86 / 100 / 48.25.
- The range quoted for LLF leaves this case out.
- E7 (`e7_overloaded_priority.csv`): LLF reaches 99.98–100% priority, 100% mission sufficiency and ≈100% non-priority at **every** priority share from 30% to 90%. AQPS-RT-ET reaches 93.9–99.4% priority. Table 6 (L633–658) omits LLF.
- E5b: in 45-EV runs, LLF ≥ AQPS-RT-ET on all service metrics at 2:1, 3:1 and 4:1.
- E3: LLF priority fulfilment is 94.54%, close to AQPS-RT-ET's 95.08%, with 99.8% non-priority against 46.3%.

**Fix**
- Give the full ranges.
- Include LLF and EDF in Table 6.
- State plainly where LLF dominates: low congestion, and the 45-EV S2 and E7 settings.
- Discuss why: the 45-EV S2 and E7 scenarios aren't congested enough for LLF to fail. Consider adjusting them, or say so.

### B4. The Layer-2 QP in code does not implement Eqs. 1–10 as printed
| Paper (`main.tex`) | Code (`src/aqps/tou_optimization.py`) |
|---|---|
| Eq. 3: H^NC = (Σ_t E_t^agg − Σ_i E_i^req)², one aggregate term | `H_NC = Σ_i w_i (delivered_i − target_i)²` with hidden weights **w = 5.0 (priority) / 0.35 (non-priority)** (L119); not in the paper |
| E_i^req = remaining session requirement | `target_i` is a **horizon-pro-rated target**. For non-priority vehicles it is `max(min_needed_in_h, e_rem·w/dep_rel)` (L116); for priority vehicles, the maximum deliverable in the 6-h horizon |
| Eq. 8: x_min = max(x_session, 3.84 kW) | `min(3.84, p_max, average rate needed)`, then **scaled down** so column sums ≤ 0.85·P_site (L130–131) and row energy ≤ 0.95·E_rem (L136–137) |
| Eq. 9 (≤ E_req) for non-priority only | `delivered ≤ e_rem` for all vehicles. Acceptable as a battery-headroom bound; L168 states this |
| Eq. 10 (≥ E_req) for priority, hard | Not a constraint at all; only the soft quadratic penalty. Priority fulfilment therefore settles at about 96% |
| "Layer 0 uses Eq. 10 … as the exact feasibility criterion" (L168, L202) | Feasibility = `max predicted shortfall ≤ 0.5 kWh` against the horizon-limited target (`priority_selection.py` L30, L94) |
| Horizon H, departure-aware | Fixed `planning_horizon_steps = 24` (6 h at 15 min), in `run_all_experiments.py` L125 and `scheduler.py` L29 |
| Eq. 5: three-phase branch limits | Not implemented. The WP0 decision (L = 1, so Eq. 5 reduces to Eq. 6) was not applied to the text |

**Fix:** either implement Eqs. 1–10 as written, or rewrite Eqs. 3, 8 and 10 and their text to describe the code exactly. Plan hard constraint #1 says the equations should stay unchanged, so implementing them is preferred:
- aggregate H^NC, or a hard Eq. 10 for the priority set;
- the full 3.84 kW floor;
- a horizon that reaches departure;
- state L = 1 for Eq. 5.

### B5. α₃ makes the regulariser dominate the tariff
**Evidence**
- Paper L131: "α₃ = 0.005 is chosen small enough that the Tikhonov regulariser conditions the QP Hessian without overriding ToU price arbitrage".
- Marginal of H^EC per kW per step = C_t·Δt = 0.32 × 0.25 = **0.08** at peak.
- Marginal of α₃·H^QC = 2·α₃·P_agg = 0.01·P_agg, which is **1.0 at 100 kW**: about 12× the peak price signal.
- Shifting 1 kW from a peak step to an off-peak step saves 0.035 in H^EC. The QC term opposes any load difference above about 3.5 kW between the two steps. The optimiser is therefore close to a pure load-flattener.
- **Observed effects**
  - AQPS-RT-ET in 45-EV S1 spends **$145 in the peak band**, against $35 for LLF.
  - Offline-Optimal costs more than LLF at equal energy in S1 ($197 vs $181 for 45 EVs; $450 vs $409 for 90 EVs).
  - The E6 "plateau" for α₃ ∈ [5e-4, 1e-2] is flat because H^QC dominates across the whole range, not because the design is robust.

**Fix**
- Rescale. For example, normalise H^QC by P_site² or use α₃ ≈ 1e-5 to 1e-4.
- Check that the plan shifts load from peak to off-peak, and extend the sensitivity sweep down to small α₃ so it shows the trade-off.
- Rewrite the justification of the weights from the dimensional argument (cost per kW per step).
- R1.2 and R2.3 asked exactly this, so it must be right.

### B6. Proposition 1 (strict convexity, unique optimum) is false
**Evidence (L306–308)**
- The stated Hessian is 2α₂Δt²(I ⊗ 11ᵀ) + 2α₃(11ᵀ ⊗ I).
- The null space of the first term = charging patterns whose per-vehicle totals are zero.
- The null space of the second term = patterns whose per-step totals are zero.
- Their intersection is non-trivial whenever N_c ≥ 2 and H ≥ 2. Example: x = [[+1, −1], [−1, +1]].
- So the Hessian is singular: the problem is convex but not strictly convex, and the minimiser is not unique (only the optimal value is).
- The proof also uses a per-vehicle H^NC (I ⊗ 11ᵀ). That matches the code, not Eq. 3 as printed; Eq. 3 is rank one.

**Why it matters:** R5.4 and R2.5 asked about convexity. An incorrect proposition invites rejection.

**Fix:** restate it as "a convex QP over a non-empty compact polytope; an optimal solution exists and the optimal value is unique". If uniqueness is needed, add a small ε‖x‖² term and prove strict convexity for that version.

### B7. Proposition 2 overclaims, and Tier 3 contradicts it
**Evidence**
- L312: "guaranteed to depart with at least E_i^mission … provided the following three **necessary and sufficient** conditions hold". No proof is given. Condition 3 ignores the EVSE-count limit, and condition 2 depends on Tier 3's behaviour.
- **Tier 3 can disconnect below E_mission.**
  - Code guard (`queue_manager.py` L213): `laxity > dt OR current_energy ≥ next_trip + reserve`.
  - Paper text (L224): "E_trip + r".
  - Both **omit the return energy E_ret**, so a vehicle can be rotated out holding less than E_mission.
  - The laxity branch alone does not guarantee the vehicle is re-admitted in time.
  - This contradicts "without force-disconnection below E_i^mission" and plan §2.2, where step 3 was "otherwise disconnect nobody; escalate".
- Tier 4 text (L225), "escalate if every connected priority vehicle has zero slack", doesn't match the code (`L229`). The code escalates whenever no Tier-2 or Tier-3 candidate qualifies, including when the ATC comparison fails.

**Fix**
- Require E_curr ≥ E_trip + E_ret + r in the Tier-3 guard, or remove Tier 3.
- Restate Proposition 2 as **sufficient** conditions with a short proof sketch.
- Make the Tier-4 text match the code.

### B8. The ablations don't support the novelty claims
**Evidence**

| Claim | Data |
|---|---|
| RT (real-time Layer 0 plus safe release) improves priority service | AQPS-static ≥ RT-ET on priority fulfilment in every E1 S2/S3 case (static 98.7–100%, RT-ET 95.9–97.2%), in E3 (99.95% vs 95.08%) and in E4 (99.96–99.98% vs 95.86–96.52%). Mission sufficiency is 100% for both in E1, E3 and E4. RT's only measurable gain is about 1–4% cost. |
| Event-triggered MPC saves computation | Outcomes are identical for every T_max (E5a). Layer-2 solves: 59 → 52 (−11.9%) in the 90-EV S2 figure. **Total QP solves: RT 98 → RT-ET 139 (+42%)** in 90-EV S2, and 94 → 137 in 45-EV S2, once Layer-0 bisection and the 3 preview solves are counted. |
| The ATC score drives selection | E6: k_atc ∈ [0.5, 3] changes priority fulfilment by ≤ 0.03 points; it has no measurable effect. |
| RT recovers mission sufficiency under overload | True only in E7 at the 50% share (static 95.5% → RT-ET 100%); at 65–90% both are equal. This is the one genuine positive result for RT. |

- Run previews only on operator events or escalations, not on every PV deviation.
- Design scenarios where real-time recalculation matters: intra-day roster changes that make static flags wrong. Current injections change one vehicle each, and 3 of the 4 cases leave the outcome unchanged.
- Treat the E7 50% result as the evidence, and tone down the other claims.

## 2. Major

### M1. MPC-only is not "Layer 2 alone"
`src/aqps/baselines.py` L120 builds `TOUOptimizer(..., alpha2=15.0)`, against 100 for AQPS. The paper (L374) says it "executes the Layer 2 receding-horizon convex QP (Eqs. 1–9)". This confounds the ablation. **Fix:** use the same optimiser configuration. MPC-only also loses to LLF in uncongested S1 (91.7% vs 100%), which reviewers will question; that mostly reflects B4 and B5.

### M2. Offline-Optimal is not an upper bound
- `baselines.py` L205: only `P_agg ≤ min(grid, M·22)`. **There is no limit on the number of vehicles connected at once (≤ M)**, so this is a relaxation.
- L219 and L229: different objective (weights 10/0.5, α₂ = 150, no 3.84 kW floor).
- It is beaten on cost at equal energy by LLF in S1 (B5).
- The paper calls it "Oracle Upper Bound" (L375), and `gap_to_offline_opt_pct` is computed against it.
- **Fix:** use the same objective; add the EVSE-count limit (a mixed-integer program) or call it a "relaxed perfect-information benchmark"; report the gap on energy and on $/kWh.

### M3. E4 cannot show PV robustness
With the gross site limit (Eq. 6), PV affects cost only. Every method's service metrics are identical across all six days. The AQPS-static vs RT-ET cost gap is a constant ≈ $9.66 on every day, independent of forecast error, so the "PV deviation" trigger adds nothing measurable. **Fix:** present E4 honestly as cost sensitivity, or add a PV-dependent mechanism (for example a net-import limit), which would require a WP0 decision.

### M4. The 26.3% solve reduction is cherry-picked
The text (L571) quotes 26.3% from **45-EV S1** (80 → 59) in a paragraph about the **90-EV S2** figure, where the reduction is 11.9% (59 → 52). The cover letter and abstract use "up to 26.3%". Total solves rise (B8). **Fix:** report both Layer-2 and total solves per scenario.

### M5. Escalation counts contradict "avoiding alert fatigue"
`queue_manager.py` L229 appends the waiting vehicle on every Layer-1 call. E1 shows 43–140 escalations per day, E5b up to 687, E7 up to 352. The paper claims the preview suppresses minor alerts (L260). **Fix:** count unique escalation episodes per vehicle, send each episode to the operator once, and report alerts per day.

### M6. Rotations counted as safe releases
`queue_manager.py` L222 also appends rotated vehicles to `safe_released_p`. The "Safe Rel." columns in Tables 3–6 therefore mix Tier 2 and Tier 3. **Fix:** separate the counters and the columns.

### M7. Safe-release condition (c) is hard-coded
`queue_manager.py` L93: `overnight_return_idx = max(departure_idx + 8, 60)`, a fixed 2-h trip or 21:00 return, not the roster's return time. The paper's Eq. 15 refers to a_j^return from the roster. **Fix:** put the return time in the vehicle data and use it.

### M8. The Layer-0 implementation differs from the paper
- `priority_selection.py` L94: feasibility is checked only on `candidates[:M]`. Bisection beyond M always tests the same slice, so "exact feasibility oracle" (L202) is overstated.
- L73: `tardy_boost = 1 + 0.2·max(0, −s)` is not in Eq. 12.
- There are floors on p_i (0.15) and p̄ (0.25).
- L144: candidates are restricted to `day_ahead_priority OR w ≥ 2.0`, which is not described.
- `scheduler.py` L159: AQPS-static uses `day_ahead OR atc_score ≥ 0.35`, an undisclosed threshold.

**Fix:** document these or remove them; describe AQPS-static exactly.

### M9. Silent solver fallback; solver not recorded
- `tou_optimization.py` L169 and `baselines.py` L232 try MOSEK, then Clarabel, then OSQP, swallowing all exceptions.
- L198 falls back to a greedy heuristic, `status="fallback_heuristic"`.
- Neither the solver used nor the number of fallbacks is logged or reported.
- The paper states "All optimization subproblems are solved using the MOSEK interior-point solver" (L365) and quotes "MOSEK solve times".
- **Fix:** make the solver a config setting with no silent fallback; log the solver and status for every solve; report fallback counts; add the solver and version to the manifest.

### M10. Results are not traceable
- `.gitignore` L51 (`results/`) excludes raw data, summaries and manifests.
- The manifest has no solver, CVXPY version or CPU model.
- The only evidence is `all_summaries_bundle.txt`.
- **Fix:** commit `results/*/summary/*.csv` and `manifest.json` (and the raw files if under 100 MB, or Git LFS), as in plan §4a.

### M11. Self-extracting build and fix scripts
- `build_aqps_revision.py` (3,262 lines), `fix_solver_and_warnings.py`, `fix_physics_and_remove_v1.py` and `build_epsr_overleaf_zip.py` contain full copies of source files as strings.
- Running them overwrites `src/` and **deletes `results/`, `figs_epsr/` and `tables_epsr/`** (`shutil.rmtree`, `fix_*.py` L13 and L19).
- This leaves two sources of truth and a risk of data loss.
- **Fix:** delete them once their content is in `src/`; work on feature branches with PRs.

### M12. False statement about non-priority service
L459: non-priority fulfilment is 35–50% "before non-priority vehicles resume charging in the evening off-peak window". The metrics are end-of-day values, so there is no later recovery. **Fix:** delete the clause; report the non-priority energy shortfall in kWh and its consequence.

### M13. Template mismatch
`\documentclass[final,3p,times,twocolumn]{elsarticle}` together with `\usepackage{epsr}` (which forces a 150 mm single-column geometry with double spacing) gives a hybrid layout. The agreed choice was the single-column preprint. **Fix:** `\documentclass[preprint,12pt]{elsarticle}` + `epsr.sty`, as in the WP8 stage-1 zip.

### M14. Simulation scope differs from the plan
- One day, 06:00 to 06:00 (96 × 15 min).
- The next-day roster (`next_day_dep_idx`, `next_day_req_kwh`) is used only inside safe-release condition (c). It is not simulated.
- The 15-min step and 6-h horizon differ from the planned 5-min step and horizon to departure.
- `driver_unavailable` caps departure at index 95 (`run_all_experiments.py` L222).
- **Fix:** either extend the simulation or state the scope plainly in §4.1 and the limitations.

### M15. AQPS-v1 removed
`fix_physics_and_remove_v1.py` removed the submitted method. Without it the paper can't show what the revision adds over the rejected version. **Fix:** optional for a new submission, but recommended: rerun v1 (with the double-count fix) on the new data.
